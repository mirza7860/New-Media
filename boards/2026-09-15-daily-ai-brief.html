TOP STORY · AGENTS / ENTERPRISE

Salesforce moves enterprise agents from chat sessions toward persistent work

Confirmed company announcement

On September 14, Salesforce announced a new portfolio of job-ready Agentforce agents and a long-horizon runtime designed to pursue goals across days and weeks. The runtime adds memory, durable execution and dynamic steering; Salesforce also announced multi-agent orchestration and an Agent Optimizer. Casey, its help agent, was described as generally available, while some other capabilities remain pilot or planned for general availability.

Company-reported metrics

Salesforce says Agentforce and Slack have delivered 7 billion Agentic Work Units, including 3.2 billion in Q2, and cites customer-reported outcomes such as 50% of Engine inquiries resolved by an agent and 90% of core Hibbett shopper journeys handled by AI. These are vendor/customer claims, not independent measurements.

Analysis / inference

The meaningful shift is architectural: the enterprise agent is being positioned as a durable process, not a single-turn assistant. Memory plus durable execution turns “ask AI” into “assign AI a goal and let it keep working,” which raises the importance of permissions, auditability, failure recovery and human approval.

02 · FINANCE / AGENTS

Anthropic takes Claude deeper into wealth-management workflows

Confirmed launch

Anthropic announced Claude for Financial Advisors on September 14, connecting Claude to custodians, portfolio platforms, CRMs and planning tools. The product is aimed at research, meeting preparation, portfolio work and documentation. Reuters independently reported the launch and named integrations including BlackRock, Charles Schwab, Addepar, Envestnet, iCapital, Orion, Wealthbox, Wealth.com and Zocks.

Analysis / inference

This is verticalization at the workflow layer rather than a new foundation model. The competitive unit is becoming the model plus trusted data connectors plus domain-specific skills plus compliance controls. That is harder to replicate than a generic chatbot UI and gives incumbents with distribution a strong route into regulated work.

03 · SAFETY / GOVERNANCE

Microsoft publishes a Humanist AI code of conduct amid the frontier-safety debate

Confirmed announcement

Microsoft AI published a first draft of a Code of Conduct for its MAI models on September 14 and opened it for public consultation. The framework says AI should remain subordinate, aligned and contained, and sets boundaries around harmful content, weapons assistance, offensive cyber activity, mass influence, child exploitation, nonconsensual deepfakes and self-harm. Microsoft says the draft is intended to guide both model development and deployment.

Status

This is a draft for consultation, not a regulation or independently audited safety standard. The rules therefore represent Microsoft's proposed operating principles rather than an external guarantee about model behavior.

Analysis / inference

The notable change is that safety positioning is becoming product and training policy. Coming directly after Anthropic's public call to pace frontier development, Microsoft's move adds another major lab to a conversation about explicit capability boundaries and external coordination.

04 · INFRASTRUCTURE / STARTUPS

Buildots raises $130M as AI data-center construction becomes a software market

Confirmed company announcement

Buildots announced a $130 million funding round on September 14, bringing total capital raised to $297 million. Its platform uses computer vision to compare construction-site imagery with schedules and 3D models, identifying completed work, deviations and potential delays. Buildots says it is deployed by more than 100 large firms, including data-center owners and contractors.

Independent cross-check

SiliconANGLE independently reported the financing and described the platform's construction-monitoring workflow. The investor announcement from Intel Capital also confirms the $130 million round and the stated $297 million total.

Analysis / inference

The AI infrastructure boom is creating second-order software markets around the physical buildout. The interesting opportunity is not another model provider; it is operational intelligence that compresses schedule risk, inspection effort and coordination costs while thousands of data-center and industrial projects scale.

05 · RESEARCH / ROBOTICS

MIT's HardFlow targets hard constraints in safety-critical generative AI

Research announcement

MIT News reported September 14 that researchers developed HardFlow, an algorithm intended to help generative models satisfy non-negotiable constraints in robotics, physical control and computer vision. The approach gives the model more freedom during generation and enforces hard constraints on the final output; MIT reports that experiments consistently satisfied required constraints while finding better solutions than existing methods.

Analysis / inference

The research matters because reliable generative AI for physical systems cannot be judged only by average output quality. A system that is “usually right” is insufficient when violating one hard safety constraint can cause failure. Constraint-aware generation is therefore a promising bridge between generative modeling and safety-critical control.

Primary: MIT News — New method enables AI for safety-critical situations — September 14, 2026
06 · INFRASTRUCTURE / SECURITY

Fortaegis puts hardware-rooted security on the AI infrastructure funding map

Reported financing

The Financial Times reported September 14 that Amsterdam-based Fortaegis completed a $50 million Series A led by Serendipity Capital, with participation from TEL Venture Capital. The company is developing semiconductor security technology based on physical properties of silicon, targeting AI infrastructure, defense, telecoms and autonomous systems.

Company claim

Fortaegis says its technology generates changing encryption keys from chip-level characteristics and is designed for low-latency secure compute. Those technical and deployment claims are company assertions; the financing is independently reported by the FT.

Analysis / inference

As agents gain access to real systems, identity and execution security become infrastructure primitives. Hardware-backed trust can matter where software credentials, model endpoints and cloud boundaries are themselves part of the attack surface.
