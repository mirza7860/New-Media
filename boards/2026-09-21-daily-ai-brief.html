U.S. and China move toward a formal AI safety dialogue

Original event: September 20, 2026, New York · Reporting published September 20–21, 2026

Reported fact: U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said U.S. and Chinese officials had discussed establishing an AI dialogue focused on national-security concerns and a notification mechanism for AI-related incidents that rise to that level. The proposal was made during Bessent's September 20 talks with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng ahead of the Trump–Xi summit. Reuters reported that the Chinese response to the proposal was not clear; China's Xinhua readout acknowledged that the sides discussed AI but did not describe a matching commitment.

Analysis / inference: This is more consequential than another voluntary AI-safety statement because it attempts to create a state-to-state incident channel between the two countries that dominate frontier AI development. It is not yet an operational treaty, binding safety standard, or evidence of deep technical cooperation. The important signal is institutional: AI incidents are increasingly being treated as a national-security coordination problem rather than only a technology-policy issue.

Event: Sep 20 · Reuters publication: Sep 20 · FT follow-up: Sep 21

Analysis: The immediate change is diplomatic, not technical. If institutionalized, the channel could become an early-warning mechanism for frontier-model incidents, misuse and other AI risks.
